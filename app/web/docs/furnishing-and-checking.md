# Furnishing and checking

*Filling rooms the way the game's own are, dressing shelves and tables, lighting a place, and finding what's wrong.*

## Auto-furnish a whole place

Open an interior on the World map and press **🪑 Auto-furnish rooms…** (Place panel).

- Each room is listed with its size and **what it's for** (bedroom, kitchen, storeroom, shop, shrine…), read from
  its name. Change any of them, or pick **none** to leave a room empty.
- **Furnish as** sets how well off the owner is: **Poor** (sparse rooms, bare walls, cheap things), **Modest**,
  **Comfortable** (the usual), **Rich**, **Exquisite**; **By trade or faction…** (a smith's forge, an apothecary's
  bench, a priest's shrine, a guard's weapon rack); or **Leave loot worthless**, nothing worth more than the gold
  you set, for places where free loot would spoil things.
- **Furnish like** takes one of your **examples of good design** as the model (below).
- **Hang things on the walls too**: tapestries, banners, paintings, sconces. **Only decorate the walls** adds no
  furniture, for rooms that are already furnished.
- If Auto-furnish has been here before, **Take out the things Auto-furnish put here before** stops rooms
  doubling up.

It all goes in as **one change**: one Undo takes it back. The furniture comes from similar places in your load
order: beds and shelves against the walls, tables with chairs, things on the tables.

**Plants** come the game's way too: as many floor planters as the game's rooms of that kind and size hold, at its
spots (a post's foot, under a window, beside a doorway, a corner, by a seat), with one of the game's own gardens in
each; pots on tables and shelves and hanging planters at its rates. When a room is too full for a floor planter by
the time the planting comes, a pot stands on the furniture instead.

The first time, Auto-furnish waits for the Workshop to finish learning from your game's places, and shows a bar
while it does.

## Make it lived in

**🏕 Lived in** (Place panel) leaves what people leave round the place's beds, bedrolls, fires, tables and work
spots: bowls, cups and bread by a bedroll, a ladle and plates at the fire, prongs and a hammer at the anvil, papers and
a quill at a desk. Each is one of the game's own arrangements round such a spot, from a place of the same kind
(a cave, a camp, a house), turned with your spot and set down on what's under it. Spots with things round them already
are left as they are. One Undo. The first time it learns from your game's places (about a minute).

**Camps:** a cave, tomb, tower or other dungeon with too few beds for who lives there (the band, or two at least)
first gets the game's own camps of that kind: a bedroll with its fire, log, stool, screen or table, as the game's
bandits, smugglers or Daedra worshippers set theirs, laid whole on clear floor away from the walls, the doors and
each other. Then it's made lived in round them.

**Stories:** a dungeon also gets the game's own scenes of what happened there, as many as its places of that kind
hold: a fallen adventurer (remains with their weapon, armour, a note or some gold), an offering at an altar, or a
pile of bones. Each is laid whole on clear floor, from a place of the same kind (a tomb's dead among ash urns and
prayer stools, a cave's skeleton with its sword), and the band's own first. A place telling three stories already is
left as it is.

Populate does the same for the people it places (**lived in**, on by default), picking by who they are.

## One room, one piece

Right-click in a room (its floor, a wall, or a thing in it):

| Entry | What it does |
|---|---|
| 🪑 Furnish the *room* (the game's way) | Just this room; the others aren't touched. |
| 🎲 Furnish the *room* again | Lays it afresh, taking out what was laid before. |
| 🪑 Furnish the *room* as… | As another kind of room. |
| 🪄 Dress everything in the *room* | Every shelf, table and counter dressed the game's way, one Undo. |
| ✂ Remove the *room* | For a room built from a plan: its pieces and furniture come out, the wall closes again. |

Right-click a piece of furniture:

- **📚 Fill with books** (shelves and bookcases): books that fit the shelf, standing or laid flat the way the game
  does it.
- **➕ Add one thing**: one more thing that belongs on it.
- **🫳 Settle it**: loose things fall onto what's under them and rise out of what they're sunk into. **…tipping it
  over if it can't stand** lets things that can't stand fall over. **🫳 Settle everything on it** does a whole table.

## Examples of good design

On a place's Place panel, **☆ Use as an example** marks one of the game's places you like. Auto-furnish then
weighs it five times a plain place when it learns how rooms are furnished, and **Furnish like…** can take it as
the model.

## Lighting, fog and water

An interior's look is in the **Sky** panel:

- **Start from a mood…**: Cosy house, Shop or guild hall, Tavern at night, Cave, Ancestral tomb, Dwemer ruin,
  Daedric shrine, Mine, Sewers, Telvanni tower, Bright and plain.
- **Ambient** (light everywhere), **Sunlight** (light from one direction, giving things shape), **Fog** colour and
  **Fog thickness**.
- **Has water at height**, **Behaves like outdoors** (weather and sky show inside, as in Mournhold), **Players
  can't sleep here**.
- **Copy from another place…** takes another place's settings.

Changes show in 3D straight away; **Save** keeps them. In the game they apply the next time you enter.

## Find gaps

**🔧 Find gaps…** (Place panel) looks for the spots in a place built from room pieces where you can see out into
the void: open sides onto nothing, doorways that lead nowhere, missing ceilings, back walls, posts standing in the
open. Each is marked on the map and in 3D. Fix them one by one, or all at once.

**Fix here** (right-click a building piece or an open tile edge): the Workshop reads that joint and offers what
the game stands there, most often first. Rest the mouse on one to see it in place; a click lays it;
**…and every spot like this one** lays it wherever the place has the same situation.

## Check this place

**🩺 Check place…** (Place panel) lists what's wrong, grouped by kind, each with a marker and **Show me**:

- things floating, or through a wall or the floor;
- chairs with nothing to sit at;
- shelves, tapestries and lights hung on nothing;
- books leaning on nothing;
- the gaps Find gaps looks for;
- and whether the place **serves its purpose**: a smith with no forge, a tavern with no bar or tables, a temple
  with no shrine, a shop with nothing to sell from, a home with no bed, people with nowhere to sleep, bare rooms,
  doors inside that lead nowhere, an interior that reaches outside its building, and stairs nobody can use (every
  flight is walked from its foot to its head: a wall across either end, a piece closing over it). These have no
  button: the fixes are **Auto-furnish rooms** (pick the kind), the door's **Leads to**, *Interior from a building*
  and *Roll the interior*.

Where there's an obvious fix (set it down, pull it out of the wall, remove it), it has a button. Nothing changes
until you click a fix, and one Undo takes a fix back.

## Walking paths

People in a place walk along its **path grid**. After building or furnishing, **🚶 Paths → Make them for me** works
out a set that keeps clear of the furniture. See *The World map* for editing them by hand.
