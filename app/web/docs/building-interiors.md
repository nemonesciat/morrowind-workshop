# Building interiors

*Houses, shops and halls from the game's own wall, floor and stair pieces: laid out on a plan, built out piece by
piece, or fitted inside a building outdoors.*

## Which way to build

| You want… | Use |
|---|---|
| A whole new house, laid out room by room | **Create → Build from pieces** (the room planner) |
| A copy of a place the game made, with your name | **Create → Build from pieces → Plans ▾**, or **Copy a place from the game** |
| One more room on a place you built | Right-click an outer wall → **Add a room through this wall** |
| To build out by hand, piece by piece | **⬚ Extend** in the 3D view |
| An interior that fits a building standing outdoors | Right-click the building → **🏠 An interior for this building…** |
| A building put together piece by piece, to set down outdoors | **Library → 🔨 Prefab workbench** |

The Workshop builds the way the game's own builders did. It learnt that from your game's interiors the first
time it ran (see *How the Workshop learns*), so a few tools wait for that and say so.

## The room planner (Create → Build from pieces)

1. **Name** the place. Morrowind's style is *Town, Owner's House*; it's the name shown on the door.
2. Pick a **Style**: Hlaalu, Redoran, Telvanni, Imperial, Nord, Common… The ones no place in your game uses are
   hidden (tick to show them).
3. **Lay out the rooms** on the grid (below).
4. Set the **Finish**: the light (dim, normal, bright, dark), **Hang lanterns**, **Furnish the rooms**, and
   **Furnish as** (how well off the owner is).
5. **Outside door**: where its door goes outside (the World map's view, the spot you right-clicked, or where your
   character stands in game). Outdoors you can have **Just the door** on a wall already there, or **A whole new
   building in this style** with the door on it, facing the way you pick.
6. **Build it.** One Undo takes it all back.

### Laying out rooms

| Do | To |
|---|---|
| **+ Room ▾** | Add a room: Bedroom, Kitchen, Hall, Storeroom, Study, Shop, Shrine… at the game's usual size for it. |
| Drag a room | Move it. Drag its corners or the tabs on its sides to resize it. |
| Click the wall between two rooms | Put a doorway in it, or take it out. |
| Click an outside wall | Put the way out there. |
| The floor tabs | Up to five floors: Top floor, Upstairs, Ground floor, Cellar, Lower cellar. Double-click a tab to name it. |
| **3D** | Every floor stacked like a doll's house; drag to turn it. |

- **A room's name says what it's for**: Auto-furnish reads it ("Nemo's room" is a bedroom; the plan shows the
  kind when the name doesn't say it).
- **Stairs** aren't a room: put rooms on another floor and stairs are added for you, from the style's own stair
  pieces. Drag a flight where you want it: only that flight moves, and it stays exactly where you let go. Where it
  can't stand, it turns red and the panel says why. **R** turns it; **✦ Pick a spot for me** has the Workshop pick one, once. Stairs never
  move by themselves: adding or changing rooms leaves every flight where it stands.
- **One stairwell** (three floors or more): each flight stands over the one below the way the game's stairwells do,
  turning back and forth in two columns.
- **A Stair hall** (**+ Room ▾**, or any room with "stair" in its name) keeps every flight inside it, so the stairs
  stay out of the bedrooms and libraries. Three tiles wide fits every style.
- **Plans ▾** has ready-made layouts, your saved plans (**💾 Save this plan…**), and **🏘 Plan from a place in the
  game**: one of the game's houses read as a plan, to study and build from.
- **↶ ↷** undo and redo on the plan; **🗑 Clear plan** starts again from one room.

### Copying a place the game made

**Copy a place from the game** copies a room or house whole into a new place with your name: **Keep** everything,
the walls and lights only, or bare walls. Its front door is linked to a new door outside, picked from the doors
the game uses on that style. People and creatures are never copied.

## Adding to a place you built

- **Add a room through a wall:** right-click an outer wall of a place built from pieces. A see-through room
  appears beyond it; pick its size (Room, Hall, Corridor, Closet), kind and name, then **Build**: only the new room
  and the doorway are laid (and furnished), the rest stays as it is.
- **⬚ Extend** (3D toolbar, indoors): every open face of every building piece glows as a strip (gold; red where
  you'd see out into the void). Click a strip: a column of candidates slides in, what the game joins to that face
  most often first. Hover one to see it in place, click (or Enter) to lay it; **Tab** cycles, **Backspace** takes
  the last piece off. Above the candidates are the patterns the game repeats from there (a run of walls, a
  hallway's segments and junctions in turn, a piece with its ring, a Redoran dome with its walls and corners).
  **Close it** lays the pieces that close what you built; on a dome's centre, its whole ring. **Esc** leaves, and
  all the steps become one change.
- **Snap pieces** (3D toolbar, Snap ▾) lines up walls and floors with their neighbours as you move them;
  **↻ Other pieces** (Selected panel, or right-click) offers the other pieces of the style that fit the same spot.
  In 3D they're tried on the spot: **← →** step through them, **Enter** keeps one, **Esc** puts the piece back.
  Good for a stair, a ceiling or a wall that clips.

## An interior for a building outdoors

Right-click a building's wall or door outdoors → **🏠 An interior for this building…**. The Workshop checks the
building is closed and sound, draws the floors the interior would have over it on the map, and **Build** makes
an interior that fits its outline (about as roomy inside as the game's own), with its door and the building's
linked both ways.

- **Floors** and **Cellar** (None, Usual, Big: the game's own sizes for the style; a cellar can reach past the
  walls, under the ground).
- The rooms are cut the way the game's own storeys are: a big room and smaller ones round it. **🎲** gives
  other rooms in the same space.
- **Furnish** (Bare, or how well off), **Light** and **Lanterns**: it is furnished and lit as it is made.
- A building with a shell the game makes a ready-made interior for (a shack, a hut, a tent, a Telvanni pod) offers
  two ways. **Ready-made per shell** makes a place for each such shell with a door outside, copied from the game's
  own. The list shows each place and which game interior it is like (**⌖** shows the piece). **Planned to fit**
  plans one interior for the whole building instead, floor by floor.

**✎ Open in the planner** (planned interiors) opens the plan in Build from pieces, to change its rooms, doorways and
stairs; **Build** there makes it inside the building, its door linked.

**🔍 Check this building** looks it over by the game's own rules for its style: pieces standing on nothing, gaps
in the walls, the roof.

### Roll the interior

Right-click a building that has an interior already (a town's, or one you built) → **🎲 Roll the interior…**, to
try other interiors for it until one is right, without losing the old one. The card shows the interior as it is
(**before**) and each roll as a chip with the purpose check's verdict (✓ serves its purpose, ✗ a smith with no
forge, a home with no bed…); the picked one's plan (its rooms, where the forge, beds, counter and doors are), what it
holds, and its faults.

- **🎲 Roll** plans and furnishes another interior for the building, in its style, and the building's door leads
  into it. Four rolls are kept at a time; click a chip to look at another, **👁 Walk in** to see it in place.
- **Furnish as** picks what it is for (a home, a smithy, a tavern, a Mages' hall…) and how well off.
- **Roll until it has** a forge, a bed, two floors or no fault rolls again on its own, up to eight times, and stops at
  the first that passes.
- **✓ Keep this one** locks it in: the door leads there, the old interior's people move in (their records, lines and
  daily routines untouched; where they stand and what they own follow the new rooms), the old interior and the other
  rolls go, the walking paths are made, and the place takes the old name. One Undo takes it all back.
- **↶ Put the old one back** takes every roll out.
- **Find the faulty interiors in this town** lists every mod-made interior in the cell the purpose check faults, each
  with a 🎲 to roll it.

The interior and its people are two layers: a roll never makes people. To give a rolled place new people instead of
the old ones, keep it, then **Populate** it.

## The prefab workbench

**Library → 🔨 Prefab workbench** (or **Ctrl+B** anywhere, right-click **Library**, or Ctrl+K "workbench") opens an
empty space with a floor grid and its own camera.

- Pick a style; its pieces are on the shelf on the left. Or pick **All pieces, by ID** and type an ID or its start
  (`ex_imp_wall`, `t_imp_set`): every piece that has it, in ID order (statics and doors at first; the buttons over the list add lights, containers
  and the rest). Drag them in. They **snap** the way the game joins
  them, else flush with the piece beside them. A door or window dragged to a frame, doorway or window hole drops
  into it. **🚪 Fill openings** fills them all the game's way.
- The same shelf works in the World map and 3D view: **▤ Panels → Pieces**. Click a piece, then click where it goes,
  or drag it into the view.
- **Moving** (toolbar): **Joins & flush**, **Joins only** or **Free**; hold **Alt** while dragging to move freely
  once. For fine moves: the **arrow keys** (1 unit, Shift 16), **Page Up / Down**, or type **X Y Z**. **Turn 15°** /
  **Turn free** sets the turn ring's steps.
- **Lining up while you drag:** besides meeting faces, a piece **centres** on the piece it's against, lines its
  sides up with the other pieces round it standing the same way, or comes to the **middle of a gap** between two
  (a door between two walls). A pink line shows what it lined up with. The same works in the 3D view.
- **⇹ Align** (toolbar, or right-click with several selected): lines the selection up on the piece the handles sit
  on: **centre** it along red, green or in height, **line up** a side, the bottoms or the tops, or **space evenly**
  (three or more). It goes along the handles' axes, so with **⟀ Own axes** it follows a turned piece. In the 3D view,
  select several and right-click **⇹ Align them…**: they line up on the last one picked, as one Undo.
- It's **checked as it grows**, with marks on what's wrong.
- Fly with **W A S D** (Q / E down and up, Shift faster); right-drag looks, middle-drag pans. Left-drag on empty
  space selects several. **[ ]** turn by 15° (Shift: 1°); the **Turn**, **Tilt** and **Size** boxes are exact;
  **⊥ Straighten** undoes a tilt. Right-click for a menu.
- **✛ World axes / ⟀ Own axes** (toolbar): the handles move and turn a piece along the world's lines or along its
  own sides. The selected piece always shows its own axes: red its length, green across, blue up.
- **Ctrl+D** puts a copy of the selection straight down beside it, end to end along the selected piece's own red
  axis (**Shift**: the other way); press again to carry the row on. **Ctrl+M** puts a mirrored copy against the
  selection's far side across X (**Shift**: across Y), for the other half of a symmetrical building.
  **Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V** copy and paste where you click. Mirroring swaps a left-hand piece for its right-hand twin when
  the game has one (a stair, a corner); one without is turned to match.
- **⋯ Array…** (right-click) makes many copies at once: in a row along the piece's red or green axis or upwards, end to
  end with a gap you choose, or round a ring a radius in from the piece (a round hall, a tower's wall).
- **H** hides the selection, **Shift+H** shows it alone, **Alt+H** shows everything again: to work inside a building.
  Hidden pieces are still part of it.
- The toolbar: **Grid** puts a moved piece that joins nothing on a grid of that step; **About the piece / About the
  middle** is what several pieces turn about; beside the Turn box, the selection's size (across × deep × high).
- **💾 Save these as a prefab…** (right-click) keeps just the selected part, a porch or a tower's top, in the Library.
- **👁** on a shelf piece shows the game's buildings that use it.
- Anything that can stand in the world can go on the bench from **Browse**: **🔨 Add to the workbench** puts it in
  the middle of the bench's view, selected.
- When it's done, set it down in the world in one go: it follows the mouse as one ghost on the land, a click
  places it, and one Undo takes it back. What's on the bench stays there between sessions.

## The Style palette

**🎨 Style palette…** (Place panel, or **🎨 Style** in 3D) sorts building pieces, furniture and decor by theme
(Hlaalu, Redoran, Telvanni, Velothi, Nord, Imperial, the Legion, Gold Coast…), the way the game's own places use
them:

- **🧱 Shell**: walls, corners, floors, ceilings, doorways, stairs, columns.
- **🪑 Furniture** and **🕯 Decor**.
- **🧩 Sets**: what the game puts together, such as a table with its chairs or a bed with its chest. A set goes down
  whole, as one Undo.

**🔍 All** (first in the list) searches every style's palette at once; each result shows the style that has it
most. A style's palette holds what the game uses in that style often enough to count (in at least 3 of its places and
3% of them), so a thing rare in every style is in none; All then lists it below, dimmer, under **📚 Catalogue**, from
the whole catalogue. **⇲** on the palette's title bar docks it as a panel, beside Place or wherever you drag its tab; its panel
menu (⋮) has **Float it as a window**. It opens the way you left it.

Under **Regions**, each region has its own palette (◆ the open place's): **🪨 Rocks & cliffs**, grouped by set
(the models of one family the game uses together), and **🌿 Trees and plants**, learnt from the region's own land.

Staples come first. Rest the mouse on a piece to see what the game stands next to it; click to place it, or drag
it into the map or the 3D view.

Something the Workshop didn't learn in a style can be added by hand: **＋ Add…** in the palette finds anything in
your load order, or **🎨 Add to a style…** on its page in Browse. To add many at once, Shift-click or Ctrl-click
them in Browse's list, then **🎨 Add to a style…** (or **🌿 Add to a brush…**) on the bar above it. It's listed first in its group with a green
**yours**; right-click it to take it back out. Your additions are kept apart from what the Workshop learns.

**＋ New style** (under **Your own** in the palette's list) makes a style of your own: add anything to it with
**＋ Add…**. Tick your prefabs under **Its town buildings**, each with what it is (house, shop, tavern…), and pick
whose towns it is planned like; then **Like ★ your style** in a town's panel (Land) builds the town of them. A prefab
needs a door: its outermost door is its front.

## Hidden doors and secret entrances

Right-click a placed object (a wall, a bookcase, a log pile):

| Entry | What it makes |
|---|---|
| 🚪 Make this a door to… | It keeps its looks and spot but leads somewhere: a place that exists, a new one copied from the game, or one built from pieces. |
| ↪ Rotating panel… | A door that swings open on the edge you choose. |
| ⇆ Sliding panel… | A panel that slides aside, used directly or pulled by another object (a lever, a book). |
| ▭ Make this an ordinary object again | Takes it back. |

Each is one change.

## After building

- **Auto-furnish rooms…**, **Find gaps…** and **Check place…** in the Place panel: see *Furnishing and checking*.
- The interior's lighting, fog and water are in the **Sky** panel.
- **Play here** to walk round it in the game: see *Playing and testing*.
